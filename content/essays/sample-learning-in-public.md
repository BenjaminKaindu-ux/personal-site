There is a version of learning that happens quietly, and a version that happens out loud. I've done both, and the second one is harder — but it's also the one that sticks.

## Why write it down at all

When I finish a notebook or a tutorial, the feeling is always the same: I understood it in the moment. Then the next week proves I understood it *as it was being explained to me*, which is a different thing entirely.

Writing is the difference. Not for an audience — for my own memory. An essay is a spell-check for understanding.

## A quote worth keeping

> “If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough.” — attributed to many

Whether or not it's really Feynman, the instruction is right. Notes are where I explain things to a future me who has forgotten the context.

## What good notes have in common

- They start with a *why*, not a *what*.
- They include the mistakes, not just the answer.
- They are written six days after learning, not six seconds after.

A tiny Python snippet to help me remember:

```python
# notes that age well include the failure mode
def remember(concept, mistake, fix):
    return {"concept": concept, "mistake": mistake, "fix": fix}
```
